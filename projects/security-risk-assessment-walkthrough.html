Governance, risk, and compliance / Lab walkthrough

Run my security
risk assessment lab.

Practice the full GRC cycle: find control gaps, map them to NIST SP 800-53, rate the risk, and turn findings into a tracked remediation plan.

NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5FIPS 199PCI DSSRBACPOA&M

Before you start

  • A copy of NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5 (free from csrc.nist.gov).
  • A spreadsheet for your risk register and POA&M, and a document for your policy.
  • A practice scenario. Mine included a point-of-sale (POS) environment.

What you’ll learn

  • Security control assessment and gap analysis
  • Rating impact on confidentiality, integrity, and availability
  • Risk-based remediation planning
  • POA&M tracking
  • RBAC and least-privilege design
  • Writing a policy aligned to PCI DSS

Step by step

  1. Scope your system

    Describe a practice organization and the system you’re assessing: what it does, the data it handles, and who uses it.

  2. Categorize it with FIPS 199

    Rate the potential impact of a loss of confidentiality, integrity, and availability as low, moderate, or high. The highest of the three sets the overall category.

  3. Identify control gaps

    List the deficiencies you find and map each one to NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5. My lab focused on these controls:

    • AC-6 Least Privilege: users have more access than their job needs.
    • CA-5 Plan of Action and Milestones: findings are not tracked to completion.
    • CA-7 Continuous Monitoring: security status is not watched over time.
    • RA-3 Risk Assessment: risks are not formally identified and rated.
    • RA-7 Risk Response: there is no defined way to respond to findings.

  4. Rate the risk

    For each gap, note its impact on confidentiality, integrity, and availability, and how likely it is to be exploited. Use that to rank what to fix first.

  5. Write remediation strategies

    For each control, write a risk-based remediation strategy that explains what changes, why it reduces risk, and who owns it.

  6. Recommend RBAC and access reviews

    Design role-based access so people only get what their role needs, and add periodic access reviews to catch excessive access to sensitive information.

  7. Build a POA&M tracker

    Create a Plan of Action and Milestones to track remediation to completion. My tracker covered risk severity, ownership, deadlines, status, and evidence. Suggested columns:

    text
    ID | Weakness | NIST control | Risk severity | Owner | Milestones | Due date | Status | Evidence of completion

  8. Write a PCI DSS-aligned policy

    Write a security policy for a point-of-sale environment that covers network security controls, secure configurations, changing default credentials, and malware protection. Name a technical owner for each requirement.

Check your work

  • Every gap is mapped to a specific NIST SP 800-53 control.
  • Each finding has a CIA impact rating and a priority.
  • Every POA&M item has an owner, a due date, a status, and evidence.
  • Your policy names an owner for each requirement.

Interview ready

Be ready to explain

  • How you used FIPS 199 to decide how critical the system is.
  • Why you chose AC-6, CA-5, CA-7, RA-3, and RA-7, and how they connect.
  • How a POA&M turns findings into accountable, trackable work.
  • How RBAC and access reviews reduce excessive access.
  • How continuous monitoring (CA-7) ties to detection work like my Sentinel lab.

This lab sparked real conversations in my interviews. Know your work front to back and you’ll be ready for “walk me through it.”
